How To Choose The Best Home Care Services For Your Parents

Choosing the right home care services is an important decision for families who want the best support for their loved ones — whether care is needed at home or during a hospital stay.

By the SIDDHI ElderCare team · 3 min read

Home / Resources & Guides

The best home care for your parents is the care that fits their daily life, not the provider with the longest list of services. Whether support is needed at home, during a hospital stay or after discharge, a few honest questions will help your family decide with confidence.

Start with what your parents actually need

Before you call anyone, write down what a normal week looks like for your parents. Then sort their needs into three groups:

  • Medical: medicines, doctor visits, physiotherapy, dressing wounds, injections.
  • Daily living: bathing, meals, moving around safely, companionship.
  • Practical: transport, bills and paperwork, groceries, deliveries.

Some families need mostly practical help and regular check-ins. Others need a trained nurse or attendant. Knowing which is which keeps you from paying for care you don't need, and from missing care you do.

Involve your parents in this step. Ask what they want help with and what worries them. A parent who feels heard accepts help far more easily than one who feels decided for.

Know the difference between types of care

Non-medical care covers companionship, errands, hospital coordination and regular well-being checks. Medical care means qualified nurses and doctors carrying out clinical tasks, usually on a doctor's advice. Many families need a mix of both.

A good provider is clear about what it does itself and what it arranges through qualified professionals. If an answer to "who does the medical part?" sounds vague, ask again.

Questions to ask every provider

  1. Who exactly will care for my parents, and how are they verified? Ask about ID checks, references and police verification.
  2. What training do your caretakers and nurses have?
  3. What happens at 2 a.m. if something goes wrong? You want a real phone number and a clear idea of how fast someone can respond.
  4. How will I stay informed? Regular updates by call or WhatsApp matter, especially if you live far away.
  5. What if the caretaker is unwell or leaves? Ask how continuity is handled.
  6. What will this cost, in writing? Get clarity on what is included and what is extra.
  7. Can we start small and change the plan later? Avoid long lock-ins in the beginning.

Red flags to watch for

  • Vague answers about who will visit or how they were verified.
  • Pressure to sign up on the spot.
  • No clear contact for nights, weekends or emergencies.
  • Promises to do everything, including clinical care, with no qualified staff to back it.
  • Pricing that is unclear or keeps changing.

Meet first, then start small

Speak to the care coordinator before committing, ideally with your parents in the room. Watch how the coordinator talks to them, not just to you. Respect, patience and plain language tell you a great deal.

Begin with a short trial. After the first week, ask your parents how it felt and check in with the caretaker. Comfort and trust matter as much as skill, and both take a little time to build.

If you live far away

When you can't be there in person, reliability matters more than any single service. Look for a named point of contact, regular updates you don't have to chase, and someone who can be at the hospital or the doctor's clinic when you can't.
